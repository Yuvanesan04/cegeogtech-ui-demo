From industrial by-product to building material

A geopolymer is a binder made by activating silica and alumina rich powders with an alkaline solution. Four stages describe how it forms.

A simplified overview of geopolymerisation. Details vary with the raw materials and mix design.
  1. 1

    Select the source materials

    By-products and clays rich in silica and alumina, such as fly ash, slag and metakaolin, are the starting powders.

  2. 2

    Activate with alkali

    An alkaline solution attacks the surface of the particles and releases silicate and aluminate species into the mix.

  3. 3

    Form the network

    Those species reconnect into a three-dimensional aluminosilicate network as the mixture sets.

  4. 4

    Apply the hardened binder

    The hardened binder goes into construction products, coatings, ceramics and other materials that our researchers test and develop.
